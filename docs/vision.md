# LifeRPG — Product Vision

## 1. What is LifeRPG?

LifeRPG is a mobile gamified personal development application that turns real-life progress into an RPG experience.

The user's real life becomes the game.

Instead of simply tracking habits, tasks, goals, or achievements, LifeRPG represents personal development through a character, skills, quests, experience points, achievements, items, and a growing virtual world.

The main idea is simple:

> Develop your real life. Develop your character. Develop your world.

---

## 2. The Core Concept

LifeRPG connects real-life actions with game progression.

A user performs meaningful actions in real life:

- studying
- working
- programming
- learning languages
- exercising
- walking
- cleaning
- completing courses
- achieving personal goals
- developing creative skills
- taking care of themselves
- completing important life tasks

These actions can become quests and provide experience points, skill progression, achievements, and rewards.

The application should make personal development feel visible, rewarding, and enjoyable.

---

## 3. The Player

The player is represented by a customizable RPG character.

The character can have:

- name
- appearance
- hairstyle
- clothing
- accessories
- mood
- energy
- level
- experience
- skills
- achievements
- badges
- inventory
- companions
- personal status

The character represents the user's development rather than being a completely separate fictional hero.

---

## 4. The Player's Story

Every user has their own life story.

LifeRPG can gradually transform important parts of that story into a personal game world.

The user may record:

- important events
- achievements
- education
- certificates
- diplomas
- career milestones
- personal goals
- travels
- skills
- hobbies
- meaningful experiences

This creates a personal timeline and a visual representation of the user's journey.

---

## 5. Quests

Real-life actions can become quests.

Examples:

- Study programming for 30 minutes.
- Practice German for 20 minutes.
- Go for a walk.
- Drink enough water.
- Clean your room for 10 minutes.
- Complete a course lesson.
- Work on your portfolio.
- Apply for a job.
- Exercise.
- Read a book.
- Do something creative.
- Leave the house and visit a café.
- Give someone a genuine compliment.

Quests should range from very small actions to major personal goals.

The goal is not to force productivity.

The goal is to make progress visible and rewarding.

---

## 6. Skills

LifeRPG represents different areas of personal development as skills.

Possible skill categories include:

- Programming
- Languages
- Education
- Career
- Health
- Fitness
- Creativity
- Social
- Finance
- Discipline
- Hobbies
- Travel
- Personal Development

Skills can gain experience through completed quests and real-life achievements.

---

## 7. Experience and Progression

Completed actions provide experience points.

Experience contributes to:

- character level
- individual skills
- achievements
- unlockable items
- world progression

The progression system should reward consistency and meaningful progress rather than simply rewarding the user for doing as many tasks as possible.

---

## 8. The Personal World

Every player has their own virtual world.

The world represents the user's personal development.

As the player progresses, new elements of the world can become available:

- rooms
- furniture
- decorations
- gardens
- buildings
- locations
- shops
- companions
- travel destinations
- new areas

The world should visually communicate:

> "This is what my real-life progress has built."

---

## 9. Rewards and Collection

Players can collect virtual items and achievements.

Possible rewards include:

- clothing
- furniture
- decorations
- pets
- companions
- badges
- trophies
- special items
- seasonal items
- travel souvenirs

Some rewards can be connected to real-life achievements.

---

## 10. Personalization

LifeRPG should allow users to make their experience personal.

Users should be able to customize:

- character appearance
- home
- world
- items
- categories
- goals
- skills
- quest types
- visual themes

The application should feel like the user's own world rather than a generic productivity tracker.

---

## 11. Gentle Guidance

LifeRPG may provide optional suggestions for healthy and balanced activities.

Examples:

- Take a short walk.
- Take a break.
- Drink some water.
- Spend time outside.
- Do something creative.
- Contact a friend.
- Take time to recover.
- Try something new.

These suggestions should be supportive rather than judgmental.

LifeRPG is not intended to replace professional psychological or medical advice.

---

## 12. AI Features

AI may become an important part of LifeRPG in the future.

Possible AI features include:

- quest suggestions
- personalized recommendations
- conversational assistance
- story generation
- personalized challenges
- progress summaries
- character or world suggestions
- language-learning interactions
- adaptive encouragement

AI should support the user's experience rather than control it.

---

## 13. Real-Life Items and Memories

In future versions, users may be able to add real-life objects, photos, memories, and travel experiences to their virtual world.

For example:

- a photo from a trip
- a certificate
- a favorite object
- a travel memory
- a personal achievement

Some of these elements could be transformed into collectible in-game objects.

Advanced image processing or AI-based transformation may be explored later.

---

## 14. Languages and Cultural Themes

LifeRPG should initially be developed with English as the primary application language.

The architecture should support localization so that additional languages can be added later.

Potential languages include:

- English
- German
- Ukrainian
- Russian
- French

The application may also include optional themed communication styles and playful language packs.

Examples could include:

- fantasy themes
- magical themes
- humorous styles
- fictional-inspired communication styles

These should be implemented as original themes and should avoid unauthorized use of copyrighted characters or protected intellectual property.

---

## 15. Visual Direction

The initial visual direction is inspired by cozy retro RPG and pixel-art games.

The desired feeling is:

- cozy
- nostalgic
- playful
- colorful
- personal
- relaxing
- rewarding
- indie-game-like

The visual style should remain practical enough for an indie project and manageable for a solo developer.

---

## 16. Core Gameplay Loop

The central gameplay loop is:

Real life
↓
Action
↓
Quest
↓
Completion
↓
Experience
↓
Skill progression
↓
Achievement / Reward
↓
World development
↓
Motivation for the next real-life action

The real world drives the game.

The game visualizes the real-world progress.

---

## 17. Product Philosophy

LifeRPG should not turn life into an endless productivity competition.

The application should support different kinds of progress:

- productivity
- learning
- health
- rest
- creativity
- social connection
- hobbies
- exploration
- personal goals

Rest and recovery are also valid forms of progress.

The user should feel encouraged rather than punished.

---

## 18. MVP Principle

LifeRPG is a large concept and cannot be built completely at once.

The first version should focus on the smallest playable experience that demonstrates the core idea:

> Real-life action → Quest → XP → Skill → Reward → World progression.

Advanced features such as AI, image transformation, social systems, travel systems, complex economies, and large open worlds should be developed only after the core gameplay loop works well.
