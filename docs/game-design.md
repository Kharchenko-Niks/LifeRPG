# LifeRPG — Game Design

## 1. Core Gameplay Concept

LifeRPG turns real-life actions into RPG gameplay.

The player performs meaningful activities in real life and converts them into game progress.

The core gameplay loop is:

Real-life action
↓
Quest
↓
Quest completion
↓
XP and skill progress
↓
Level / achievement / reward
↓
World progression
↓
New goals and quests

The game should encourage real-world development rather than replacing it.

---

## 2. The Player Character

The player is represented by a customizable RPG character.

The character has:

- Name
- Appearance
- Level
- Experience
- Mood
- Energy
- Skills
- Achievements
- Badges
- Inventory
- Equipment
- Companions

The character represents the user's personal development.

---

## 3. Experience Points

Experience points (XP) are the main progression currency.

Players earn XP by completing quests and meaningful real-life activities.

XP contributes to:

- Character level
- Skill progression
- Achievements
- Unlockable rewards
- World progression

XP should reflect the difficulty and significance of an activity.

---

## 4. Quest Difficulty

Each quest has a difficulty level.

### Easy

Small actions that require little time or effort.

Examples:

- Drink water
- Make the bed
- Clean for 10 minutes
- Take a short walk

Suggested reward:

10–30 XP

---

### Medium

Activities that require more time, effort, or concentration.

Examples:

- Study programming for 30 minutes
- Practice a language
- Exercise
- Complete a work task
- Read a chapter of a book

Suggested reward:

30–70 XP

---

### Hard

Activities that require significant effort or concentration.

Examples:

- Complete a large project task
- Study for several hours
- Complete an important work task
- Finish a difficult assignment

Suggested reward:

70–150 XP

---

### Major

Important milestones or achievements.

Examples:

- Complete a course
- Pass an exam
- Complete a portfolio project
- Get a job
- Reach an important personal milestone

Suggested reward:

150–500+ XP

---

## 5. Quest Categories

Quests can belong to different areas of life.

Initial categories:

- Health
- Fitness
- Education
- Career
- Programming
- Languages
- Finance
- Creativity
- Social
- Hobbies
- Home
- Personal Development
- Travel
- Recovery

Users should be able to select the categories that are relevant to their lives.

---

## 6. Quest Types

### Daily Quests

Small activities that can be repeated regularly.

Examples:

- Drink water
- Practice German
- Walk
- Study
- Exercise

---

### One-Time Quests

Tasks that are completed once.

Examples:

- Finish a project
- Submit an application
- Buy something important
- Complete a course

---

### Long-Term Goals

Large objectives divided into smaller quests.

Example:

Goal:

"Become a Junior Frontend Developer"

Possible quests:

- Build portfolio
- Finish React course
- Create project
- Improve CV
- Practice interview questions
- Apply for jobs

Completing smaller quests contributes to the larger goal.

---

### Optional / Discovery Quests

Activities designed to encourage exploration or positive experiences.

Examples:

- Visit a new café
- Explore a new place
- Try a new hobby
- Talk to someone new
- Visit a museum
- Take a photo during a walk

These quests should be optional and supportive.

---

## 8. Character Level

The character has a global level.

Global XP is earned from completed quests.

Example:

Level 1
0 XP

Level 2
100 XP

Level 3
250 XP

Level 4
450 XP

The exact progression formula will be designed later.

The level system should make progress visible without encouraging unhealthy competition.

---

## 9. Achievements

Achievements represent meaningful milestones.

Examples:

- First Quest
- First Week Completed
- First Skill Level Up
- First Course Completed
- First Project Completed
- First Job Application
- 10 Quests Completed
- 1000 XP Earned
- First Travel Memory
- Programming Level 5

Achievements can provide:

- Badges
- XP
- Items
- Decorations
- Titles
- Unlockable content

---

## 10. Rewards

Rewards make progression visible and enjoyable.

Possible rewards:

- Clothing
- Accessories
- Furniture
- Decorations
- Pets
- Companions
- Badges
- Titles
- Special items
- World decorations
- New locations

Rewards should primarily provide personalization and visual progression.

---

## 11. The Personal World

Every player has a personal virtual world.

The world develops as the player progresses.

Early progression may begin with:

- A small room
- A simple house
- A small outdoor area

Later progression can unlock:

- More rooms
- Furniture
- Garden
- Workshop
- Shops
- Roads
- Buildings
- New locations
- Travel areas
- Larger parts of the world

The world represents the player's development.

The goal is to make the player's progress visually meaningful.

---

## 12. World Progression

World progression can be connected to player milestones.

Example:

Complete several quests
↓
Earn XP
↓
Level up
↓
Unlock new furniture
↓
Decorate the house

Major achievements can unlock larger changes.

Example:

Complete a major education goal
↓
Unlock a study room

Complete a career milestone
↓
Unlock a workspace

Complete a travel achievement
↓
Unlock a travel souvenir or location

This system will be expanded in future versions.

---

## 13. Mood and Energy

The character may have temporary states such as:

- Mood
- Energy
- Recovery

These values should represent the user's current state without becoming a strict health-monitoring system.

The system may use them to suggest different types of activities.

Example:

Low energy:

- Take a break
- Drink water
- Take a short walk
- Rest

Higher energy:

- Study
- Exercise
- Work on a project
- Complete a challenging quest

The system should remain supportive rather than punitive.

---

## 14. Recovery

Recovery is considered a valid part of progression.

The player should not be required to be productive constantly.

Possible recovery activities:

- Rest
- Sleep
- Walk
- Relax
- Spend time with friends
- Watch a movie
- Read
- Take a break

Recovery-related activities may provide small amounts of progress or restore character energy.

---

## 17. Future AI Integration

AI may later assist with:

- Quest generation
- Goal breakdown
- Personalized recommendations
- Progress summaries
- Conversational assistance
- Story generation
- Character development
- World suggestions
- Language practice

AI should operate as an optional assistant.

The core gameplay should remain functional without AI.

---

## 19. Core MVP Gameplay Loop

The first playable version should focus on:

1. Create a character
2. Create or receive a quest
3. Complete the quest
4. Receive XP
5. Increase a skill
6. Level up
7. Unlock a reward
8. Improve the personal world

This loop is the foundation of LifeRPG.

Everything else can be built around it later.
